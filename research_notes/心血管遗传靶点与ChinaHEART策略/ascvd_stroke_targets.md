# Human-genetics-validated drug targets for ASCVD (CAD/MI) and stroke (ischemic subtypes + ICH), status as of 2026-09-27

Scope note: I checked 2025–2026 trial readouts against company press releases, SEC filings and trade/society news (TCTMD, ACC, ESC, NEJM abstracts), searched on 2026-09-27. Landmark genetics and older trial papers are cited by DOI, using facts that are well established. The local evidence base (/data/yy_data/radar/background/cardio-omics/report.md) focuses on AI and PRS. Its one relevant target item is an LPA GRS paper (UKB, 43-SNP LPA GRS, Lp(a) ≥120 nmol/L as elevated; JAMA Cardiol 2020, doi 10.1001/jamacardio.2020.5398).

## Q1. Which targets have genetic support AND outcome-trial evidence, and what is their 2025–2026 status?

### Takeaway
Only the LDL axis (HMGCR, NPC1L1, PCSK9, and LDLR, which all three act through) has a closed loop: human genetics predicted the effect, outcome trials proved it, and the drugs are approved. Evolocumab now also has primary-prevention outcome data (VESALIUS-CV, AHA Nov 2025).

2026 brought two major genetically motivated failures:
- **Pelacarsen (LPA ASO), Lp(a)HORIZON:** missed its primary MACE endpoint (topline 2026-09-04).
- **Ziltivekimab (IL-6 ligand), ZEUS:** HR 0.99 (topline 2026-07-31).

Factor XIa inhibition has its first positive phase 3 result: asundexian in OCEANIC-STROKE, a 26% cut in recurrent ischemic stroke with no excess major bleeding.

Still pending:
- CETP (obicetrapib PREVAIL): interim analysis Q4 2026, results expected Q1 2027.
- Olpasiran OCEAN(a)-Outcomes: completion expected ~Dec 2026.
- Lepodisiran ACCLAIM-Lp(a): ~2029.
- Milvexian LIBREXIA-AF and LIBREXIA-STROKE: topline expected 2026, not verified as reported.
- APOC3/ANGPTL3 cardiovascular outcomes trials: not reported.

### Cited Findings

**LDL axis (HMGCR / NPC1L1 / PCSK9 / LDLR): genetics and trials are concordant**
- PCSK9 nonsense variants (African Americans) lowered LDL-C by 28% and CHD risk by 88%. This is the canonical LoF-to-target example — [Cohen 2006 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa054013)
- NPC1L1 inactivating mutations lowered LDL-C and CHD risk (~53% lower odds) — [MIGen/Stitziel 2014 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1405386). Ezetimibe added to simvastatin reduced MACE modestly — [IMPROVE-IT 2015 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1410489)
- PCSK9 mAb outcome trials were positive in secondary prevention — [FOURIER 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1615664); [ODYSSEY OUTCOMES 2018](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1801174)
- **VESALIUS-CV (AHA, Nov 2025):**
  - Design: n=12,257, no prior MI/stroke, LDL-C ≥90 mg/dL plus atherosclerosis or high-risk diabetes; 774 sites, 33 countries; median follow-up 4.6 y.
  - Results: 3-point MACE −25%; 4-point MACE −19%; first MI −36%.
  - CV death −21% and all-cause death −20% were not formally tested because of the hierarchy.
  - Significance: first PCSK9 inhibitor with event reduction in both primary and secondary prevention.
  - Sources: [ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/clinical-trials/2025/11/20/21/09/vesalius-cv); [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2514428); [TIMI slides](https://timi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/The-Effect-of-EVolocumab-in-PatiEntS-at-High-CArdiovascuLar-RIsk-WithoUt-Prior-Myocardial-Infarction-or-Stroke-Primary-Results-of-the-VESALIUS-CV-Study.pdf)
- Minikel 2024 uses HMGCR as its cardiovascular example: GWAS variants at HMGCR have small effects, yet statins produce large reductions in cholesterol and CV risk. Genetic effect size does not predict drug effect size — [Minikel 2024 Nature (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096124/)
- In Chinese and UK populations, PCSK9 genetic variants were used to profile vascular and non-vascular effects, so there is a CKB-based drug-target MR for PCSK9 in East Asians — [PCSK9 variants CKB/UKB](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11144468/)

**CETP**
- Anacetrapib cut major coronary events ~9% in REVEAL, in proportion to its non-HDL/apoB lowering, not its HDL rise — [REVEAL 2017 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444). Genetic CETP-score MR showed the CHD benefit tracks the apoB reduction — [Ference 2017 JAMA](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2017.14429)
- **Obicetrapib PREVAIL:**
  - Started Mar 2022; >9,500 randomized; enrollment completed Apr 2024.
  - Originally planned readout: Q1 2026. Now: interim analysis Q4 2026, results expected Q1 2027. Status: ONGOING.
  - Source: [NewAmsterdam Q1 2026 update](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/07/3289808/0/en/newamsterdam-pharma-provides-corporate-update-and-reports-first-quarter-financial-results.html) (via [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/newamsterdam-pharma-provides-corporate-update-and-reports-first-quarter-2026-financial-results))
- The European Commission approved obicetrapib (Ubeslo) and obicetrapib+ezetimibe (Evlarco) for LDL-C lowering on 2026-09-21. This is an LDL-lowering approval, before any outcome data — [GlobeNewswire 2026-09-21](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/21/3365512/0/en/newamsterdam-pharma-and-menarini-group-receive-european-commission-approval-for-ubeslo-obicetrapib-monotherapy-and-evlarco-obicetrapib-plus-ezetimibe-fixed-dose-combination.html)
- A pooled phase 3 analysis (JACC 2025) reported MACE data for obicetrapib. These are exploratory and not a CVOT — [JACC 2025](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.07.056); TANDEM fixed-dose combination — [Lancet 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/piis0140-6736(25)00721-4/abstract)

**LPA / Lp(a)**
- LPA variants (rs10455872, rs3798220) associate with Lp(a) level and CHD — [Clarke 2009 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0902604)
- **Lp(a)HORIZON (pelacarsen, Novartis/Ionis):** topline 2026-09-04. NOT met.
  - Design: n=8,323 with established CVD. Primary endpoint evaluated in two populations: Lp(a) ≥70 mg/dL (overall) and ≥90 mg/dL.
  - Primary endpoint: CV death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke, urgent coronary revascularization.
  - Results: Lp(a) fell, but this "did not demonstrate that this translated into reduced cardiovascular risk in the overall study population." Safety acceptable. Full data at an upcoming congress. No development plan announced.
  - Sources: [Novartis PR](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-announces-lpahorizon-phase-iii-topline-results-pelacarsen-patients-elevated-lpa-and-established-cardiovascular-disease-cvd); [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/lpahorizon-top-line-results-dash-hopes-pelacarsen); [Ionis](https://ir.ionis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ionis-partner-novartis-announces-lpahorizon-phase-3-topline)

- **OCEAN(a)-Outcomes (olpasiran siRNA, Amgen):** 7,297 with ASCVD and high Lp(a); completion expected ~Dec 2026. ONGOING — [Family Heart Foundation tracker](https://familyheart.org/lpa-clinical-trials); [NCT05581303](https://clinicaltrials.ucsd.edu/trial/NCT05581303)

- MR in East Asian vs European ancestry (Circulation 2024/25) assessed Lp(a) causality for CHD and stroke types. I could not retrieve the abstract (403) — [Circulation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.124.072086)

**APOC3 and ANGPTL3 (triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, TRL)**
- APOC3 LoF carriers have ~40% lower TG and ~40% lower CHD risk — [TG and HDL Working Group 2014 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307095); [Jørgensen 2014 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1308027)

- **Plozasiran (APOC3 siRNA):** SHASTA-2 in sHTG — [JAMA Cardiol/PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38583092/). - IL-1β mAb canakinumab cut MACE ~15%, with more fatal infections; not approved for CV use — [CANTOS 2017 NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1707914)

### Inferences
- **Scorecard by target** (genetic support → outcome-trial status, 2026-09):
  - HMGCR, NPC1L1, PCSK9, LDLR: proven, approved.
  - CETP: proven in REVEAL via apoB. Obicetrapib is approved in the EU for LDL-C, but its CVOT is pending.
  - LPA: first CVOT negative (HORIZON). The class question now rests on OCEAN(a) (late 2026/2027) and ACCLAIM (~2029).
  - APOC3, ANGPTL3: pancreatitis/TG benefit shown. No ASCVD CVOT result.
  - IL6R: one negative CVOT (ZEUS, a CKD population).
  - F11: positive in noncardioembolic secondary stroke prevention; mixed in AF/ACS.
- **Why HORIZON may have failed although MR looks strong** (a hypothesis; full data not yet presented). MR estimates suggested a very large absolute Lp(a) reduction (~100 mg/dL) is needed for LDL-equivalent benefit ([Burgess 2018 JAMA Cardiol](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2018.1470)). Candidate explanations:
  - The trial may have been underpowered at the achieved absolute reduction.
  - Background LDL-C was ~66 mg/dL.
  - Follow-up was short relative to lifelong genetic exposure.
  - The MR assumption that "per-particle apoB risk" transfers to a secondary-prevention intervention window may fail.
- ZEUS vs the IL6R MR/CANTOS signal:
  - 【已更正，见专家评审 F4】 The earlier version of this line argued that the MR instrument acts through membrane IL6R/classical signalling while ZEUS blocks the ligand, implying a mismatch that could explain the null. That argument runs the wrong way. IL6R Asp358Ala (rs2228145) increases IL-6R shedding (raising soluble IL-6R) and so *weakens* membrane-bound classical signalling. Ziltivekimab neutralises the ligand and therefore blocks both classical and trans-signalling, a broader inhibition than the genetic instrument. The pathway-component difference cannot explain the null result; if anything it makes the null harder to explain.
  - Corrected reading: several explanations remain undistinguished until full data are presented. (a) Scale extrapolation: extrapolating the per-allele effect linearly on the hsCRP scale implies a large benefit, yet HR 0.99 (0.88–1.11) excludes a benefit larger than ~12%. So either hsCRP is not the causal scale for IL-6, or the effect is non-linear/saturates beyond the range the instrument covers (rule: extrapolate only within the biomarker range the instrument covers; a target-engagement marker is not the causal scale). (b) The CKD population has a high share of non-atherosclerotic CV death and competing risk. (c) hsCRP ≥2 enrichment selects "inflamed" patients, not necessarily those with IL-6-driven events. (d) Excess serious infections offset part of any benefit. CANTOS (IL-1β, MACE ~−15%) and LoDoCo2 (colchicine) were positive, so the inflammation hypothesis is not refuted; the negative result should be restricted to downstream IL-6 ligand blockade in a CKD population. Genetic support on this axis is best described as direction-consistent with an unclear magnitude scale. (Source of correction: expert panel review, `panel/final-synthesis.md` F4/D6.)
- Factor XI: genetic support (F11/KLKB1 loci; congenital FXI deficiency is linked to fewer ischemic events and mild bleeding) shows most clearly in noncardioembolic stroke on antiplatelets. Dose and comparator (apixaban vs placebo) decide the outcome. This is an intervention-layer (dose/exposure) problem, not a target-validity problem.

## Q2. What fraction of CAD genetic architecture acts through lipids/BP vs vessel-wall biology, and why are vessel-wall targets hard to drug?

### Takeaway
About half of CAD GWAS variants show directionally consistent effects on conventional risk factors (lipids, BP, glycemia, adiposity). The other half, including the strongest common locus 9p21, act through unknown mechanisms, mostly in the vessel wall (smooth muscle cells, endothelium, macrophages). These loci are mainly regulatory, non-coding, cell-state-specific and small-effect, so they have no druggable circulating mediator. That is why none has become an approved drug.

### Cited Findings
- **Aragam 2022 (CARDIoGRAMplusC4D, Nature Genetics):**
  - 181,522 cases / 1,165,690 participants.
  - 241 loci (30 new) from the primary analysis, plus 38 new from cross-ancestry meta-analysis with a Japanese GWAS: 279 variants in total.
  - 220 candidate causal genes prioritized (123 supported by ≥3 methods).
  - Clustering implicated early developmental processes, cell-cycle signalling, and vascular cell migration/proliferation.
  - Sources: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36474045/); [Nature Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01233-6)
- In that analysis, 128 (53%) of CAD variants had directionally consistent associations with conventional risk factors (lipids, BP, hyperglycemia, adiposity), so ~47% do not. For most loci the mechanism is unknown, and several act through arterial-wall mechanisms — [Nature Genetics 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01233-6); [review, PubMed 39909615](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39909615/)
- A multi-ancestry MVP GWAS of CAD (Tcheandjieu 2022) expanded loci further — [Nature Medicine 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-01891-3)
- Examples of vessel-wall loci under functional follow-up:
  - Smooth-muscle-restricted LMOD1 locus — [PMC6268002](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6268002/)
  - Multimodal CRISPR perturbation of CAD loci in endothelial cells — [PMC10047545](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10047545/)
  - PLPP3/PPAP2B regulated in macrophages via C/EBP-β — [PMC4383549](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4383549/)
  - Overview — [From GWAS to new biology and treatments in CAD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6461175/)

### Inferences
- **Why vessel-wall loci are hard to drug**, layer by layer:
  - (a) Variant→molecule: the causal variants are mostly enhancer variants, and the effector gene is often uncertain (e.g., 9p21 lies in the lncRNA ANRIL/CDKN2B-AS1 region, with no protein-coding change).
  - (b) Molecule→cell: effects depend on cell state (SMC phenotypic switching, endothelial shear response). A drug would need to modulate a transcriptional program in a specific cell state, not inhibit an enzyme in plasma.
  - (c) Cell→tissue: many such loci may affect plaque stability rather than burden, and in both directions (e.g., SMC modulation can be protective as fibrous-cap formation).
  - (d) Individual→intervention: without a circulating biomarker like LDL-C, trials cannot use surrogate endpoints, so each candidate needs a large CVOT.
- The lipid/BP loci are drug-ready because the causal chain passes through a measurable plasma molecule with a dose-response relationship, allowing surrogate-based development and genetics-based MR scaling. Vessel-wall biology lacks this "molecular handle", which structurally explains its poor tractability.
- Therefore the remaining residual-risk levers in 2026 are still mostly circulating (Lp(a), TRL/apoB, IL-6, factor XI) rather than wall-intrinsic. The 2026 IL-6 and Lp(a) failures show that circulating targets are not guaranteed either.

### Gaps
- No heritability-partitioning figure (e.g., % of SNP-h² mediated by LDL/BP) was retrieved. The 53% is a locus count, not a heritability fraction.
- No 2024–2026 update of CAD GWAS (e.g., a larger multi-ancestry CARDIoGRAM or MVP analysis with >300 loci) was verified here.

## Q3. Stroke: how does the genetic architecture differ by subtype, which targets are validated, why does ICH have few targets, and why does East Asia matter?

### Takeaway
Stroke genetics is subtype-specific:
- **Large-artery stroke** shares CAD/lipid biology: the LDL axis and HMGCR/PCSK9 targets apply.
- **Cardioembolic stroke** is driven by AF loci (PITX2, ZFHX3) and coagulation (F11/KLKB1).
- **Small-vessel stroke and ICH** share COL4A1/2, 1q22 (PMF1/SEMA4A), ICA1L and APOE (lobar/CAA), with BP as the main modifiable driver.

The validated drug targets for ischemic stroke are LDL (statins/PCSK9), antithrombotics, BP control, and now FXIa (OCEANIC-STROKE). For ICH, there is essentially no validated molecular target beyond blood pressure. There is also a genetically confirmed trade-off in East Asians: lower LDL-C reduces ischemic stroke but raises ICH (CKB MR). This matters because ICH and small-vessel stroke are far more common in China.

### Cited Findings
- **GIGASTROKE (Mishra 2022 Nature):**
  - 110,182 stroke cases (five ancestries, 33% non-European) and 1,503,898 controls.
  - 89 loci (61 new) for stroke and subtypes.
  - Putative causal genes/variants: SH3PXD2A, FURIN, GRK5, NOS3.
  - Drug targets highlighted: F11, KLKB1, PROC, GP1BA, LAMC2, VCAM1.
  - Sources: [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05165-3); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36180795/); companion drug-prioritization commentary — [Nature Cardiovascular Research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44161-022-00132-7)
- MEGASTROKE (2018; 67,162 cases) established subtype-specific architecture: e.g., cardioembolic stroke at AF loci, large-artery stroke at CAD loci — [Malik 2018 Nature Genetics](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-018-0058-3)
- **ICH genetics:** 1q22, APOE, COL4A1 and COL4A2 are implicated, predominantly in European cohorts. Large Asian GWAS remain limited — [Neurology Genetics, East Asian ICH](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/NXG.0000000000200390). 2026 review — [Kimball et al., J Cereb Blood Flow Metab 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678x241310401)
- A 2024 multitrait analysis (MTAG: ICH plus small-vessel stroke plus related traits) replicated 2q33.2 (ICA1L), 10q24.33 (OBFC1) and 13q34 (COL4A2). It reinforced APOE and added APOC1 and SH3PXD2A — [Neurology 2024](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000209666); [PubMed 39298701](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39298701/)
- Rare functional COL4A1/COL4A2 missense variants appear in sporadic ICH — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000012227). Common COL4A1/COL4A2 variation associates with sporadic small-vessel disease, especially deep ICH — [PubMed 25653287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25653287/)
- Shared cerebral small-vessel disease (cSVD) loci across white-matter hyperintensities, diffusion metrics and stroke subtypes include COL4A2, NBEAL1, ICA1L and PMF1 — [PMC12331658](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331658); [Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2023.1080168/full)
- **China Kadoorie Biobank lipid MR (Sun 2019 Nature Medicine):**
  - Nested case-control: 5,475 ischemic stroke, 4,776 ICH, 6,290 controls; 46-variant LDL score.
  - Genetically higher LDL-C raises ischemic stroke risk; lower LDL-C associates with higher ICH risk.

- FXIa: OCEANIC-STROKE shows benefit in noncardioembolic ischemic stroke without excess major bleeding (see Q1). Any-stroke HR was 0.74, so no signal of net hemorrhagic harm was reported — [ACC](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2026/04/22/11/23/oceanic-stroke)

### Inferences
- **Why ICH has few targets:**
  - (1) Variant→molecule: the main loci (COL4A1/2, 1q22, APOE ε2/ε4 for cerebral amyloid angiopathy) encode structural basement-membrane proteins or amyloid biology, not circulating enzymes.
  - (2) Tissue layer: the disease is vessel-wall fragility in penetrating arterioles (deep ICH) or amyloid-laden cortical vessels (lobar ICH). This is chronic structural degeneration that is hard to reverse pharmacologically.
  - (3) Population layer: GWAS sample sizes for ICH (~thousands of cases) are an order of magnitude below ischemic stroke, and are European-dominated.
  - (4) Intervention layer: most antithrombotic/lipid levers push risk the wrong way (the LDL-lowering/ICH trade-off, antithrombotic bleeding). ICH prevention therefore reduces to blood pressure.
- **East Asia / China:** ICH and lacunar/small-vessel stroke make up a larger share of stroke in China than in Europe. The CKB MR indicates that aggressive LDL lowering has a net stroke benefit that must be weighed against ICH risk. Small-vessel-specific loci (COL4A2, ICA1L, PMF1) and FXI (low bleeding) are strategically relevant, but East Asian ICH GWAS are underpowered — a clear opportunity for Chinese biobanks such as ChinaHEART/CKB.
- **Where the thrombosis/bleeding trade-off sits:** at the molecule→tissue layer (hemostasis vs thrombosis both need the coagulation cascade). FXI is attractive because its genetics (deficiency causes mild bleeding) suggest it is dispensable for hemostasis but important for thrombus propagation.

## Q4. What is the documented success-rate uplift for genetically supported targets, and does it hold in cardiovascular?

### Takeaway
Nelson 2015 estimated that genetic support roughly doubles the probability of approval. Minikel 2024 (Nature) refined this to 2.6× overall: 3.7× for Mendelian (OMIM) evidence and >2× for GWAS evidence, strongest in phases II–III, and largest in hematology, metabolic, respiratory and endocrine (each >3). Cardiovascular benefits but was not singled out as highest. The 2026 phase 3 failures (LPA, IL-6) and successes (PCSK9 primary prevention, FXIa stroke) fit a probabilistic uplift, not a guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- Nelson et al. 2015 estimated that human genetic evidence ~doubles the success rate from clinical development to approval — [Nelson 2015 Nature Genetics](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3314) (as summarized in [Minikel 2024 abstract, Europe PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096124/))
- **Minikel 2024 findings:**
  - Probability of success is 2.6× greater with genetic support.
  - Relative success varies by therapy area and phase and rises with confidence in the causal gene.
  - It is largely unaffected by effect size, minor allele frequency, or year of discovery.
  - Source: [Minikel 2024 Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096124/)
- **Minikel 2024 breakdown:**
  - By evidence source: OMIM 3.7; GWAS (Open Targets Genetics) >2, sensitive to variant-to-gene confidence.
  - By therapy area: hematology, metabolic, respiratory and endocrine >3; 11 of 17 areas >2.
  - By phase: the effect is largest in phases II–III.
  - Coverage: only 4.8% of active programs have germline genetic support.
  - Source: [PMC11096124](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096124/)
- In Minikel 2024, cardiovascular appears mainly through the HMGCR example: small GWAS effect, large drug effect — [PMC11096124](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096124/)

### Inferences
- **Cardiovascular 2025–2026 tally for genetically supported mechanisms in phase 3 CVOTs:**
  - Positive: evolocumab VESALIUS-CV; asundexian OCEANIC-STROKE.
  - Negative: pelacarsen HORIZON; ziltivekimab ZEUS; milvexian LIBREXIA-ACS; asundexian OCEANIC-AF (earlier).
  - Pending: obicetrapib PREVAIL; olpasiran OCEAN(a); lepodisiran ACCLAIM; milvexian LIBREXIA-AF/STROKE.
  - Most failures are explained at the intervention/population layer (dose, comparator, population, exposure duration, background therapy), not by the genetic hypothesis being wrong. This matches Minikel's finding that the uplift is largest in phase II–III efficacy but does not remove trial-design risk.
