# Why HF (HFpEF/HFrEF), AF, cardiomyopathies and vascular aging lack genetically validated drug targets — genetics, biology and bottlenecks (as of Sept 2026)

Scope note: sources below were retrieved from Europe PMC/PubMed abstracts, one full text (Henry 2025, PMC11985341), Nature Genetics/PMC pages and company/trade press for 2025–2026 pipeline events. Items marked [UNVERIFIED] come from background knowledge and could not be re-checked in this session. The local evidence base (`background/cardio-omics/report.md`) is mainly about risk prediction; the parts that matter here are the proteome-MR attrition numbers and the AF cross-ancestry GWAS.

## Q1. Heart failure: HERMES GWAS, risk-factor mediation, HFpEF as a syndrome, phenomapping, proteomics/MR

### Takeaway
Common-variant heritability for HF is low. SNP-h² is about 5% for all-cause HF, and for non-ischemic HFpEF only 1.8% ± 1.3%, which is statistically near zero. Most HF loci map onto upstream conditions (CAD, AF, BP, BMI) or onto cardiomyopathy/sarcomere biology. In HFpEF the genetic signal is almost all "risk factor" (BMI above all). Every approved drug that works in HFpEF (SGLT2i, finerenone, semaglutide/tirzepatide) came from empirical physiology or metabolic medicine, not from HF genetics. The bottleneck is at the phenotype-definition / individual → tissue layer: HFpEF is defined by an EF threshold plus symptoms, so it pools several causal routes that run through comorbidities, and the myocardium is not measured at the molecular level in living patients.

### Cited Findings
**GWAS generations (HERMES and others)**
- HERMES 2020 (Shah et al.): 47,309 cases / 930,014 controls; 12 variants at **11 loci**. **All 11** also associated with CAD, AF or reduced LV function ("shared genetic aetiology"). Loci not linked to CAD pointed to MYOZ1, SYNPO2L (cardiac development), BAG3 (proteostasis) and CDKN1A (senescence). MR showed CAD-independent causal effects of AF, BMI and hypertension. — [Shah et al., Nat Commun 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13690-5)
- Levin et al. 2022 (MVP-led multi-ancestry): up to 115,150 cases / 1,550,331 controls; **47 loci**, plus 61 loci in a multivariate analysis with CMR endophenotypes. Convergent evidence for BCKDHA / branched-chain amino acids. Proteome-wide MR found **9 circulating proteins** linked to HF or imaging traits. — [Levin et al., Nat Commun 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34216-6)
- Joseph et al. 2022: HFrEF and HFpEF have "different genetic architectures and distinct genetic association profiles", and genetic discovery for HFpEF was modest. — [Joseph et al., Nat Commun 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-35323-0)
- **HERMES 2025 (Henry et al., Nat Genet, Mar/Apr 2025)**: 1.95M individuals from 42 studies. 153,174 HF cases, of which 44,012 were non-ischemic HF (ni-HF). Of these, 5,406 had ni-HFrEF (LVEF <50%) and only 3,841 had ni-HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%). **66 loci (37 new)**. Liability-scale SNP-h²: **5.4% (HF all), 6.1% (ni-HF), 11.8% ± 2.6% (ni-HFrEF), 1.8% ± 1.3% (ni-HFpEF)**. Genetic correlation between ni-HFrEF and ni-HFpEF was 0.42. Heritability enrichment pointed to **extracardiac tissues**. In MR, SBP had its largest effect on ni-HFrEF (OR 1.92/SD) and **BMI had its largest effect on ni-HFpEF (OR 1.87/SD)**. Liability to COPD also showed a causal effect. The authors conclude the results support "HF as a multisystem clinical syndrome". Cases were 91% European ancestry. — [Henry et al., Nat Genet 2025 (PMC11985341)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-02064-3)
- **MVP + BioVU multi-ancestry subtype GWAS (Liu et al., Nat Commun, 2026)**: 38,781 HFrEF and 38,163 HFpEF cases. **46 loci for HFrEF vs only 3 for HFpEF**, with similar case numbers. All-cause HF (200,070 cases / 2.08M controls): **136 loci**. Four HFrEF loci were found only in African ancestry (near CD36, SPI1, TRIM48, SPNS3). Implicated pathways were vascular, metabolic and TGF-β/Smad. — [Liu et al., Nat Commun 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76438-y)
- Loci-count trajectory: 11 (2020) → 47 (2022) → 66 (2025, HERMES) → 136 (2026, MVP/BioVU all-cause). Sources as above.
- Mediation: I found no single up-to-date figure for "percent of HF loci mediated by AF/CAD/BMI/BP". HERMES 2020 reported that all 11 loci were pleiotropic with CAD/AF/LV function (above). See Gaps.

**HFpEF as a syndrome; phenomapping**
- Shah et al. 2015 (Circulation, published online Nov 2014): unsupervised clustering of 67 variables in 397 HFpEF patients gave phenotypically distinct groups with different outcomes. — [Shah et al., Circulation 2015](https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.114.010637)
- TOPCAT (Cohen et al. 2020) found 3 phenogroups: (1) younger with less LVH; (2) older with AF, LA enlargement, large-artery stiffness and innate-immunity/calcification biomarkers; (3) obese/diabetic/CKD with concentric LVH, high renin and TNF-α-inflammation/fibrosis biomarkers. Response to spironolactone differed by phenogroup. — [Cohen et al., JACC HF 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchf.2019.09.009)
- There is a counter-view. Packer (J Card Fail, Nov 2025) asks whether phenomapping heterogeneity should be abandoned as a route to HFpEF mechanisms and treatment. I read only the title and could not see the argument. — [Packer 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41238043/)
- Mouse model: the "two-hit" model (high-fat diet + L-NAME) reproduces HFpEF features, and implicated iNOS/XBP1s nitrosative stress. It is a comorbidity-driven model by design. — [Schiattarella et al., Nature 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1100-z)

**Proteomics / MR (from local evidence base and above)**
- UKB 52,164 people / 2,919 proteins: 636 proteins associated with any CVD, 126 with MI/IS/HF, 47 with MR support and 18 colocalized (FGF5, PROCR, FURIN highlighted). The authors state most observational associations are non-causal and several MR proteins are weakly expressed in heart or artery. — local report ref [12], `/data/yy_data/radar/background/cardio-omics/report.md`
- UKB 47,665 people / 1,459 proteins: **385 proteins associated with HF** vs 270 with CAD, 156 with AF and 21 with AS. HF has the densest plasma-protein signature, which fits a systemic, multi-organ syndrome. — local report ref [47]
- T2D subgroup (UKB, 2,198 people): 455 proteins associated with incident HF, with WFDC2 the top one. Adds prediction beyond NT-proBNP. — local report ref [53]
- In UKB, NT-proBNP gives HR 1.68/SD for MACE. — local report ref [29]

**Drugs and their origin (all empirical or metabolic, none from HF GWAS)**
- Finerenone (FINEARTS-HF, NEJM, Sept 2024): LVEF ≥40%, n = 6,001. Rate ratio for total worsening-HF events + CV death **0.84 (0.74–0.95)**. Origin: extension of MRA physiology. — [Solomon et al., NEJM 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2407107)
- Semaglutide 2.4 mg (STEP-HFpEF, NEJM, Aug 2023): n = 529, BMI ≥30. KCCQ-CSS +7.8 points vs placebo; weight −10.7 percentage points. Origin: obesity drug. — [Kosiborod et al., NEJM 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2306963)
- Tirzepatide (SUMMIT, NEJM, published Nov 2024 / print 2025): reported weight loss >20% and reduced epicardial fat. — [review summarising SUMMIT, Endocrinol Diabetes Metab 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70298). Primary: [Packer et al., NEJM 2025 [UNVERIFIED DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2410027]](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2410027)
- SGLT2i benefit across 5 RCTs in HF (meta-analysis, Lancet 2022). Origin: glucose-lowering drug whose HF benefit was found by chance in CV-outcome safety trials. — cited as ref 58 in [Henry et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-02064-3); primary [Vaduganathan et al., Lancet 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(22)01429-5) [DOI UNVERIFIED]
- Ziltivekimab (IL-6 ligand antibody): the HERMES outcome trial in HFpEF (NCT05636176) is still ongoing, with readout expected H1 2027. The sister trial ZEUS (ASCVD + CKD + inflammation) was **negative** in July 2026 (MACE HR 0.99, 0.88–1.11) despite lowering IL-6/hsCRP. — [Novo Nordisk release, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/31/3336733/0/en/novo-nordisk-provides-update-on-the-zeus-phase-3-trial-in-people-with-ascvd-ckd-and-inflammation.html); [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/zeus-trial-ziltivekimab-fails-reduce-mace-ascvd-patients)

### Inferences
- HFpEF's near-zero ni-HFpEF SNP-h² (1.8%) and 3 loci vs 46 for HFrEF at similar case numbers (MVP 2026) mean that more cases alone will not produce HFpEF-specific myocardial targets. The phenotype label captures a final common pathway fed by obesity, BP, AF, CKD and ageing. The genetic "targets" for HFpEF are largely the risk factors themselves (BMI → GLP-1RA/GIP; BP; MR activation). The empirical successes fit that picture.
- **Bottleneck layer**: individual → tissue (phenotype definition plus no molecular readout of myocardial state in living patients), and population (the comorbidity mix). In HFrEF the bottleneck sits lower. Its h² (~12%) and cardiomyocyte/sarcomere loci resemble DCM, so variant → molecule → cell mapping is workable. The limit there is translating locus biology into druggable mechanisms that SGLT2i/ARNI/MRA/BB have not already covered.
- The HF plasma proteome is dense (385 proteins) but MR-supported proteins are few (47 of 636 across CVD). This suggests most HF protein signals are consequences of disease (congestion, renal and hepatic stress), not causes. NT-proBNP-style markers measure state; they are not targets.
- Extracardiac heritability enrichment (HERMES 2025) supports looking for HFpEF targets in adipose tissue, kidney, vasculature and immune cells, not only in the cardiomyocyte.

### Gaps
- No exact published figure for "% of HF loci mediated by AF/CAD/BMI/BP" (a multivariable MR / GWAS-by-subtraction number) was found. Levin 2022 and Henry 2025 may contain one in supplements, which I did not access.
- HERMES 2025 still had only 3,841 ni-HFpEF cases, so HFpEF findings are underpowered. MVP's HFpEF definition (EHR-based) may differ from trial definitions.
- The Packer 2025 argument against phenomapping and any 2025–2026 proteomic HFpEF subtyping papers (e.g., HFpEF endotypes from SomaScan in trials) were not retrieved.
- Named HFpEF-specific protein targets with both MR and colocalization support: none confirmed in this search.

## Q2. Atrial fibrillation: loci, key genes, why no genetics-derived drugs, atrial cardiomyopathy, genotype-guided therapy

### Takeaway
AF is one of the most genetically "solved" CV traits by locus count: **>350 loci (2025)**, and 525 in a cross-population meta-analysis. Loci point to developmental transcription factors (PITX2, TBX5), sarcomere/structural genes (TTN, MYH6, SYNPO2L) and some ion channels (KCNN3). Yet no approved AF drug comes from GWAS. The main reasons: (a) the largest-effect loci act through **atrial development and structural remodeling (atrial cardiomyopathy)**, not a druggable acute electrical target; (b) the effector genes are transcription factors or giant structural proteins; (c) atrial-selective pharmacology plus ventricular proarrhythmia safety is hard to achieve. Genotype-guided therapy (e.g., 4q25/PITX2 SNPs for choosing ablation or AADs) remains exploratory.

### Cited Findings
- Roselli et al. (Nat Genet, Mar 2025): **181,446 AF cases / 1,468,899 controls**, 8 ancestry groups. **354 common loci** (135 new), 403 independent signals, plus 14 low-frequency loci. 139 prioritized genes, including CAV1, SYNPO2L, TTN, CASQ2, CAMK2D, MYH6, TBX5, PITX2, KCNN3, and new FBXO32, IGF1R, HSPB7, ABLIM1. Rare-variant burden was significant for **TTN, PKP2, FLNC, RBM20, CTNNA3**, which are cardiomyopathy genes. Pathways: muscle contractility, cardiac development, cell–cell communication. Adding PRS to CHARGE-AF gave ΔC of only 0.015–0.026 (C 0.872 in HUNT, 0.790 in UKB). The paper has no explicit drug-candidate discussion. — [Roselli et al., Nat Genet 2025 (PMC12094172)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12094172/)
- Cross-population GWAS plus proteomics (Nat Commun 2025): 252,438 AF cases, **525 loci**. — [Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61720-2)
- BioBank Japan + Europe cross-ancestry GWAS: 31 loci in the Japanese cohort (5 new), SNP-h² 6.1% (liability 11.7%). PRS transferability is limited. — local report ref [35]
- Proteomic AF score: 165 proteins, HR 2.20/SD; C-index rose from 0.771 to 0.816 over CHARGE-AF + NT-proBNP + PRS. — local report ref [33]
- PITX2 / 4q25: most studies find lower PITX2 in AF. Atrial-specific Pitx2c deletion reproduces AF electrophysiology. Different 4q25 SNPs segregate independently. Carriers of rs13143308T reportedly respond poorly to ablation or AADs (a narrative review, not an RCT). — [Jiménez-Sábado & Hove-Madsen, IJMS 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms26199780)
- Atrial cardiomyopathy (AtCM): the ESC-HFA consensus (Aug 2025) redefined AtCM as a graded disorder: atrial electrical dysfunction plus mechanical dysfunction, enlargement and/or fibrosis, with atrial failure as the end stage. It noted that inconsistent cut-offs had limited clinical use. — [Weerts et al., Eur J Heart Fail 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejhf.3782). An EJHF consensus (Aug 2026) on preventing or reversing AtCM describes AtCM as the substrate that drives AF. Current levers are risk-factor/comorbidity management, HF/valve therapies and rhythm control. Atrial-directed therapies are still "emerging". — [Zakeri et al., Eur J Heart Fail 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejhf/xuag268)
- Treating "atrial cardiopathy" without AF failed. In **ARCADIA** (JAMA, Feb 2024), apixaban vs aspirin after cryptogenic stroke with atrial cardiopathy biomarkers was stopped for futility: recurrent stroke 4.4%/yr in both arms, HR 1.00 (0.64–1.55). — [Kamel et al., JAMA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.27188)
- Genetics-adjacent AF drug efforts [UNVERIFIED in this session]: (i) SK-channel (KCNN3 locus) inhibitors, e.g., AP30663 (Acesion), in phase 2 for cardioversion; (ii) bucindolol genotype-guided by ADRB1 Arg389Arg (GENETIC-AF, 2019) was neutral on its primary endpoint. Neither has changed practice.

### Inferences
- **Bottleneck layer**: variant → molecule → cell (effector genes are developmental TFs and structural proteins, acting early in life) and cell → tissue (disease expression needs atrial fibrosis/remodeling from ageing, obesity, HF and hypertension). Many strong loci probably set atrial "developmental set-points". Inhibiting their products in adults is unlikely to reverse established substrate.
- The rare-variant overlap with cardiomyopathy genes (TTN, PKP2, FLNC, RBM20) suggests part of AF is an atrial-predominant cardiomyopathy. That argues for genotype-first sub-stratification (e.g., TTN-truncating carriers with early-onset AF) rather than one AF drug.
- Measurement bottleneck: atrial tissue is only reachable at surgery or ablation. There is no validated circulating or imaging readout of atrial fibrosis (AtCM cut-offs are inconsistent). ARCADIA suggests the biomarker definitions of atrial cardiopathy are not specific enough to guide therapy.
- The PRS increment over clinical scores is small (ΔC ~0.02). Genetics now helps more with mechanism and subgrouping than with prediction.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 status for AF drugs derived from genetic targets (SK-channel inhibitors, PITX2 modulators, anti-fibrotics). Needs a pipeline check.
- No RCT of PITX2/4q25-genotype-guided AF therapy found.
- Loci count differences (354 vs 525) reflect sample size and method. The 525-locus paper's details (loci definition, ancestries) were not verified.

## Q3. Cardiomyopathies: monogenic genes, gene therapy/editing (2024–2026), PRS modifying penetrance, and drugs

### Takeaway
Cardiomyopathy is where genetics has produced drugs, through three routes: (1) **sarcomere hypercontractility → cardiac myosin inhibitors** (mavacamten, aficamten). The mechanism was inspired by HCM genetics and is now supported by common-variant MR showing LV contractility is causal in both obstructive and non-obstructive HCM. However, mavacamten failed in non-obstructive HCM (ODYSSEY-HCM, 2025). (2) **TTR amyloid**: stabilizers (tafamidis, acoramidis), silencers (vutrisiran) and CRISPR knockout (nex-z), rooted in TTR protein genetics. (3) **Gene-specific AAV replacement** (MYBPC3, PKP2, LAMP2), now in early clinical trials. GWAS now finds 70–80 loci each for HCM and DCM. PRS clearly modifies penetrance in rare-variant carriers.

### Cited Findings
**Genetic architecture**
- HCM GWAS (Tadros et al., Nat Genet, Feb 2025): 5,900 cases / 68,359 controls plus 36,083 UKB CMR participants. **70 HCM loci (50 new)** and 62 LV-trait loci. New disease gene **SVIL**: rare truncating variants confer ~10× HCM risk. **MR supports a causal role of increased LV contractility in both obstructive and non-obstructive HCM**, "anticipating shared response to therapy". — [Tadros et al., Nat Genet 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02087-4)
- HCM GWAS meta + PRS (Lopes et al., J Med Genet, Sep 2026): 70 unique loci, new ones including MYPN, YWHAE, NOS1AP, OBSCN. PRS HR 3.19 (top 5% vs rest), 1.88/SD for HCM diagnosis in 411,213 UKB participants, stratified by sarcomere genotype. — [Lopes et al., J Med Genet 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/jmg-2026-111722)
- Multi-ancestry HCM PRS (preprint, Mar 2026): sarcomere P/LP variants explain only about one-third of HCM. Higher PRS raises risk even among sarcomere-variant carriers. — [Arora et al., Research Square preprint 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8951716/v1)
- DCM GWAS 1 (Zheng et al., Nat Genet, Nov 2024): 14,256 cases. **80 loci**, 62 effector genes (including rare-variant genes MAP3K7, NEDD4L, SSPN). Single-nucleus transcriptomics used. **PGS modifies penetrance in rare DCM variant carriers**. — [Zheng et al., Nat Genet 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01952-y)
- DCM GWAS 2 (Jurgens et al., Nat Genet, Nov 2024): 9,365 cases / 946,368 controls, **70 loci**, 63 genes, with cardiomyocyte/contractile apparatus central. PRS contribution differs by rare-variant status. MR suggests higher bodyweight and SBP are actionable causes of DCM. — [Jurgens et al., Nat Genet 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01975-5)
- The HERMES 2025 HF paper highlights monogenic cardiomyopathy genes among HF loci (BAG3 etc.), and h² of ni-HFrEF (11.8%) is about 6× that of ni-HFpEF. — [Henry et al., Nat Genet 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-02064-3)
- Canonical monogenic genes (HCM: MYH7, MYBPC3 and other sarcomere genes; DCM: TTN truncations, LMNA, RBM20, FLNC, BAG3; ACM: PKP2; Danon: LAMP2) are consistent with the rare-variant genes named in the sources above. A full curated list (ClinGen) was not re-retrieved.

**Drugs**
- Mavacamten, non-obstructive HCM (ODYSSEY-HCM, NEJM, Aug 2025): n = 580, 48 weeks. Peak VO2 difference 0.47 ml/kg/min (−0.03 to 0.98; P = 0.07). KCCQ-CSS 13.1 vs 10.4. **Primary endpoints not met.** — [Desai et al., NEJM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2505927). This conflicts with the Tadros MR prediction of shared benefit in non-obstructive HCM; how to reconcile the two is open.
- Aficamten vs metoprolol in obstructive HCM (MAPLE-HCM, phase 3, conducted Jun 2023–Mar 2025): aficamten superior on clinical endpoints and exercise capacity, regardless of prior β-blocker use. — [Lewis et al., JAMA Cardiol 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2026.1730); [Dominguez et al., JACC HF 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchf.2026.103352). SEQUOIA-HCM (NEJM 2024) and US approval of aficamten (reported Dec 2025) [UNVERIFIED in this session]. ACACIA-HCM (non-obstructive) results: not found.
- ATTR-CM: acoramidis (ATTRibute-CM, NEJM 2024) — [NEJM 2024 correspondence entry](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmc2401669); vutrisiran (HELIOS-B, 2024) — [Nat Rev Cardiol 2024 summary](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41569-024-01086-y). Tafamidis (ATTR-ACT, NEJM 2018) [UNVERIFIED in session]. Origin: TTR tetramer-stabilization biochemistry. The protective TTR T119M variant that inspired stabilizers [UNVERIFIED] is a genetics-informed mechanism, though not GWAS-derived.
- CRISPR nex-z (NTLA-2001): in ATTRv-PN (phase 1, n = 36), serum TTR fell 90–92% and stayed down through 24 months. — [Gillmore et al., NEJM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2510209). The ATTR-CM phase 3 MAGNITUDE was put on **FDA clinical hold on 29 Oct 2025** after grade 4 transaminase and bilirubin elevation. A patient died on 5 Nov 2025; the death was attributed to sepsis from a perforated ulcer. The hold was **lifted March 2026** (MAGNITUDE-2 in PN, January 2026) with extra liver monitoring. — [CGTlive](https://www.cgtlive.com/view/patient-treated-trial-intellia-transthyretin-amyloidosis-gene-editing-therapy-nex-z-dies); [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/fda-releases-hold-on-second-of-intellias-attr-cm-gene-therapy-trials/); [Intellia 10-Q Q1 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652130/000119312526215740/ntla-20260331.htm)

**Gene therapy (AAV) programs, 2024–2026**
- TN-201 (Tenaya, AAV9-MYBPC3, MyPEAK-1 phase 1b/2): interim data June 2026 at 3E13 vg/kg (n = 3, 78–104 weeks) and 6E13 vg/kg (n = 4, 26–52 weeks) showed improvement in hypertrophy measures and symptoms, with Cohort 1 benefit sustained to 2 years. EMA PRIME designation. First-in-human paper in Cardiovasc Res 2025. — [Tenaya press release, Jun 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/03/3305902/0/en/Tenaya-Therapeutics-Announces-Interim-Data-from-MyPEAK-1-Showing-Treatment-of-MYBPC3-associated-HCM-with-TN-201-Gene-Therapy-Resulted-in-Consistent-Signs-of-Cardiac-Remodeling-and-.html); [Cardiovasc Res 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cardiovascres/article/121/17/2628/8315802) (company-reported, small n, open-label)
- LX2020 (Lexeo, AAVrh10-PKP2, HEROIC-PKP2 phase 1/2): PKP2 protein rose 93% at low dose and 162% at high dose. Arrhythmia burden stabilized or improved in most of 10 participants, with no clinically significant complement activation. — [Lexeo release](https://ir.lexeotx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lexeo-therapeutics-announces-positive-interim-phase-iii-data)
- RP-A501 (Rocket, AAV9-LAMP2B, Danon disease): the pivotal phase 2 had a clinical hold that was later lifted. The protocol was modified with a recalibrated dose and rituximab/sirolimus/steroid immunomodulation. The first 3 patients showed no TMA or capillary leak, and a regulatory update is expected H2 2026. — [Rocket release](https://ir.rocketpharma.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-pharmaceuticals-announces-positive-clinical-safety-update); [CGTlive](https://www.cgtlive.com/view/rp-a501-danon-disease-gene-therapy-maintains-clinical-stability) (the hold was reportedly triggered by a patient death in 2025 [UNVERIFIED])

### Inferences
- The cardiomyopathy bottleneck is at **molecule → intervention**, specifically delivery. AAV dose-dependent toxicity (TMA, complement, liver) and cardiac transduction efficiency limit replacement or editing, and gene-by-gene programs cover small populations. TTR is the exception because the target is made in the liver, which LNP-CRISPR and siRNA can reach.
- For common-variant-driven HCM/DCM and for penetrance modification, the bottleneck shifts to **cell → tissue → individual**. PRS shows that the polygenic background (contractility, BP, BMI) drives expression. The actionable levers there are the risk factors (BP, BMI per Jurgens MR) and contractility modulation.
- ODYSSEY-HCM vs Tadros MR is instructive: a genetic causal inference at the population level (contractility → HCM *risk*) does not guarantee symptom benefit once disease is established (non-obstructive symptoms may stem from diastolic/microvascular mechanisms). This is an individual-level heterogeneity bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No verified 2026 ACACIA-HCM (aficamten, non-obstructive) result.
- No sources checked for LMNA, TTN or RBM20-specific therapies (e.g., ARRY-797/p38 inhibitor for LMNA DCM, which was stopped in 2022 [UNVERIFIED]), or base/prime editing for MYH7/RBM20 in preclinical work.
- There was no quantitative "PRS × rare variant penetrance" table (e.g., penetrance by PRS tertile in MYBPC3 carriers) in the abstracts retrieved.

## Q4. Vascular aging: arterial stiffness, CHIP, senescence — which targets exist

### Takeaway
Vascular aging has no approved target-specific drug. Arterial stiffness (PWV) has modest, BP-entangled genetics. The strongest genetically anchored "aging" target is the **CHIP → inflammasome/IL-1β/IL-6 axis**. It rests on the finding that the IL6R p.Asp358Ala variant removes CHIP-associated CVD risk, and on CANTOS TET2 subgroup data. But anti-IL-6 given without selecting for CHIP (ZEUS, ziltivekimab) was **negative for MACE in July 2026**. That makes genotype (CHIP)-stratified trials the logical next test. Senescence (e.g., CDKN1A was flagged as an HF locus in HERMES 2020) and LOY are mechanistic leads without validated drugs.

### Cited Findings
- CHIP and CVD (Bick et al., Circulation 2020): CHIP HR 1.27 for incident CVD and 1.59 for large clones (VAF >10%). **IL6R p.Asp358Ala attenuated risk among large-clone carriers (HR 0.46, 0.29–0.73) but not among non-carriers (HR 0.95; P-interaction 0.003).** — [Bick et al., Circulation 2020](https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.119.044362)
- CHIP and incident HF (UKB, JAMA Cardiol, published online 2025 / print Feb 2026): n = 417,616, 7,183 HF events. CHIP aHR 1.27 (1.15–1.40), driven by **non-DNMT3A CHIP (aHR 1.52)**: TET2, ASXL1, JAK2, spliceosome. DNMT3A aHR 1.15. CAD, AF, T2D and CKD together mediated only **28.2%** of the non-DNMT3A CHIP → HF association. — [Flynn et al., JAMA Cardiol 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2025.4603)
- CH is implicated in both HFrEF and HFpEF. Hematopoietic loss of Y (LOY) pushes macrophages toward a pro-fibrotic phenotype, whereas driver-mutation CH is pro-inflammatory, so the mechanisms diverge. — [Evans & Walsh, Immunol Rev 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/imr.70094)
- JAK2V617F CH mice: blocking IL-18 paradoxically worsened lesion growth and necrotic core. Inflammasome-branch targeting is not simply interchangeable. — [Tavallaie et al., JACC BTS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacbts.2025.101463)
- CANTOS: among 3,923 sequenced patients, TET2-mutant carriers given canakinumab had the lowest non-hematologic cancer incidence (cancer outcome). A CV-outcome effect in TET2 carriers was reported separately [UNVERIFIED, exploratory]. — [Woo et al., Cancer Prev Res 2024](https://doi.org/10.1158/1940-6207.capr-23-0342)
- ZEUS (ziltivekimab, ASCVD + CKD + hsCRP ≥2): MACE HR 0.99 (0.88–1.11), top-line July 2026, despite target engagement. The HFpEF outcome trial HERMES continues, with readout H1 2027. — [Novo Nordisk, Jul 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/31/3336733/0/en/novo-nordisk-provides-update-on-the-zeus-phase-3-trial-in-people-with-ascvd-ckd-and-inflammation.html); [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/ziltivekimab-fails-to-reduce-mace-risk-in-phase-3-zeus-trial)
- Proteome-MR for arteriosclerosis/atherosclerosis (UKB-PPP + deCODE cis-pQTL): 10 prioritized proteins. **IL6R lowers risk**, as do DUSP13, FN1 and MMP12; ANGPTL4, APOB, BRAP, LPA and ZPR1 raise it. — local report ref [43]
- Arterial stiffness genetics: a PWV GWAS in LIFE-Adult (n ≈ 3.6–6.7k) is small and addresses the causal direction between PWV and BP. — [PLoS One 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0237237)
- Neutrophil counts are causally associated (MR) with 9 CVD endpoints. Other leukocyte subtypes were inconsistent. — local report ref [46]
- Senescence: the HERMES 2020 HF locus implicates CDKN1A (p21) in cellular senescence. — [Shah et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13690-5)

### Inferences
- **Bottleneck layer**: population → intervention. The genetic evidence (IL6R × CHIP interaction) points to a *subgroup* (large-clone, non-DNMT3A CHIP), but outcome trials enroll by hsCRP/phenotype. ZEUS's null result fits the prediction that unselected IL-6 blockade dilutes the effect. A CHIP-stratified anti-IL-6/IL-1β trial is the obvious genetically-anchored test. I found none reported as of Sept 2026.
- Arterial stiffness is hard to separate genetically from BP (bidirectional causation). Large, well-powered PWV GWAS are lacking, so "vascular aging" targets are still hypotheses from mechanism (elastin/collagen, AGE crosslinks, senescence, somatic mosaicism).
- Somatic events (CHIP, LOY) are a new class of "acquired genetics" in which the target is defined by genotype but acts through immune cells. They are measurable in blood, which is the one tissue that is easy to access.

### Gaps
- No up-to-date large PWV/cfPWV GWAS (e.g., UKB arterial stiffness index) loci count retrieved.
- No senolytic RCT outcomes in cardiovascular aging retrieved.
- No confirmed CHIP-selected CV outcome trial (planned or ongoing) was found. Should be checked on ClinicalTrials.gov.

## Q5. Experimental systems best matched to each bottleneck

### Takeaway
Match the system to the layer. **iPSC-cardiomyocytes and engineered heart tissue (EHT)** suit variant → molecule → cell questions in monogenic cardiomyopathy and AF loci (with iPSC-atrial cardiomyocytes for AF chromatin). **EHT under metabolic/NO stress** is emerging for HFpEF diastolic dysfunction. **Multi-hit mouse models** (HFD + L-NAME) capture comorbidity-driven HFpEF but not human genetics. **Large biobank multi-omics plus MR** (proteome, CHIP from exomes) address the individual/population layers. None fully solves the missing in vivo readout of human myocardial and atrial state.

### Cited Findings
- iPSC-EHT HFpEF model (Cell Stem Cell, Jul 2026): human EHTs cultured with high fatty acids plus L-NAME showed impaired relaxation with preserved contraction, high BNP secretion, abnormal Ca²⁺ transients and HF-like structure. It is proposed as a human-cell platform for HFpEF drugs. — [Cell Stem Cell 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.06.007)
- iPSC-derived atrial cardiomyocytes: AF risk loci show enhanced chromatin accessibility in them, which supports their use for fine-mapping AF variants. — [Roselli et al., Nat Genet 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12094172/)
- Atrial Pitx2c deletion in mice reproduces AF-like electrophysiology. Human myocytes with the 4q25 risk allele rs13143308T show similar alterations. — [IJMS 2025 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms26199780)
- Mouse two-hit HFpEF model (HFD + L-NAME) reproduces systemic and cardiac HFpEF features. — [Schiattarella et al., Nature 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1100-z)
- Single-nucleus transcriptomics of human hearts helped assign DCM GWAS genes to cell states and pathways. — [Zheng et al., Nat Genet 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01952-y); failing vs healthy human heart expression was used to validate HF GWAS genes — [Levin et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34216-6)
- LNP delivery to liver (TTR editing) works in humans, while cardiac AAV faces dose-limiting immune toxicity (RP-A501 immunomodulation redesign). — [Gillmore 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2510209); [Rocket](https://ir.rocketpharma.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-pharmaceuticals-announces-positive-clinical-safety-update)
- JAK2V617F CH mouse models show that inflammasome-branch blockade can have paradoxical effects, so in vivo models are needed before choosing cytokine targets. — [Tavallaie 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacbts.2025.101463)

### Inferences
- Mapping each bottleneck to a system:
  - Variant → molecule (AF/DCM/HCM loci): iPSC-CM or atrial-CM with CRISPRi/a, MPRA, snATAC.
  - Cell → tissue (fibrosis, diastolic stiffness, atrial remodeling): multicellular EHT with fibroblasts, endothelium and macrophages; atrial EHT or organoids; heart-on-chip with mechanical loading. (I found no primary source on atrial organoids in this session.)
  - Tissue → individual (myocardial state in vivo): CMR (T1/ECV, strain), ECG-AI, cardiac-derived plasma proteins, cell-free DNA methylation. These are candidates, not yet validated targets.
  - Individual → population (comorbidity/syndrome): biobank-scale genotyped cohorts with serial imaging and proteomics, multivariable MR / mediation, and genotype-stratified (CHIP, TTN-truncation, PRS-high) trial enrichment.
- iPSC-CM limitations (fetal-like maturity, no systemic metabolic or immune milieu) matter most for HFpEF and vascular aging. Rodent limitations (heart rate ~10×, different atrial electrophysiology, no spontaneous AF) matter most for AF. Large animals (pig/goat AF pacing models) fill some gaps. These general points are background knowledge and were not re-sourced in this session [UNVERIFIED].

### Gaps
- No primary sources retrieved on atrial organoids or chamber-specific heart organoids (2024–2026), on HFpEF multi-cell EHT beyond the one Cell Stem Cell paper, or on large-animal AF models.
- No systematic comparison found of how well each model predicts clinical outcomes, i.e., the translational hit-rate of iPSC-CM screens in cardiology.
